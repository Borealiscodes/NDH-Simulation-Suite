# **1 — Classical Question: “Can You Hear the Shape of a Drum?”**

In 1966, Mark Kac posed the now‑canonical question:

> *Does the spectrum of the Laplace operator uniquely determine the geometry of a manifold?*

This question is framed in:

- compact Riemannian manifolds  
- fixed metric  
- rigid curvature  
- classical Laplace–Beltrami eigenvalues  
- low‑dimensional topology (typically 2D or 3D)  

The intuition:  
If you know all the eigenvalues of a vibrating drum, can you reconstruct its shape?

---

# **2 — Classical Answer: No (Milnor & Sunada)**

John Milnor (1964) and Toshikazu Sunada (1985) proved:

> **Different, non‑isometric manifolds can share the exact same spectrum.**

These are **isospectral manifolds**:

- same eigenvalues  
- same spectral multiplicities  
- different geometry  
- different topology  
- different curvature distribution  

Thus:

> **Spectral data does not uniquely determine shape.**

This is a limitation of classical spectral geometry.
